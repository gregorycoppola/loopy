# Conjecture: Backprop Learns Loop-Compensating Weights

## The Conjecture

When a transformer is trained by backprop on data
generated by a loopy factor graph, the learned FFN
weights implicitly compensate for the correlation
structure introduced by the loops.

Precisely: let G be a loopy factor graph with true
factor potentials Ψ*. Let W_BP be the pure BP weights
for G (w0 = w1 = 1, b = 0). Let W_SGD be the weights
found by backprop trained on data from G.

Conjecture: W_SGD ≠ W_BP in general for loopy G,
and the deviation W_SGD - W_BP is a function of the
loop correlation structure of G. Specifically, the
FFN weights in W_SGD are adjusted downward for
positively correlated evidence sources and upward
for negatively correlated evidence sources, relative
to W_BP.

## Why This Is Plausible

On a tree, messages arriving at a node are independent.
Pure BP weights are optimal — no correction needed.
The bayes-learner result confirms this: on a two-variable
tree graph, backprop converges to near-pure BP weights.

On a loopy graph, messages are correlated. Pure BP
overcounts shared evidence. Optimal weights compensate
by reducing the effective weight of correlated sources.
Backprop, minimizing prediction error on the true
data distribution, should find these compensating
weights.

This is consistent with the general theorem: any
weights implement weighted loopy BP on the implicit
factor graph. The learned weights are the specific
weighted BP that minimizes prediction error for the
given data distribution. On a tree this is pure BP.
On a loop it is something richer.

## The Formal Statement

Let G = (V, F, Ψ*) be a factor graph (possibly loopy).
Let D be data sampled from the true distribution P_G.
Let W* = argmin_W E_{(x,y)~D}[L(transformer_W(x), y)]
be the weights found by backprop.

Claim: W* implements weighted loopy BP on G with
factor potentials Ψ_W* that minimize the KL divergence
between the loopy BP fixed point distribution and
the true posterior P_G(· | evidence).

This would mean backprop is implicitly solving the
variational inference problem: find factor potentials
such that the loopy BP fixed point best approximates
the true posterior. This is a stronger result than
the Bethe free energy characterization of loopy BP
fixed points — it says backprop finds the potentials
that make the Bethe approximation as tight as possible
for the given graph structure.

## Relationship to Existing Results

Yedidia et al. (2003): loopy BP fixed points minimize
the Bethe free energy. This characterizes what loopy
BP converges to for fixed weights. The conjecture here
is about what weights backprop finds — a level above.

Wainwright et al. (2003): tree-reweighted BP uses
modified weights to get an upper bound on the log
partition function. The reweighting accounts for
loop correlations in a specific way. The conjecture
suggests backprop may find a similar reweighting
automatically.

Bayes-learner result (Coppola 2026): backprop finds
near-pure BP weights on a tree graph. Consistent with
the conjecture — no loops, no correction needed.

## What a Proof Would Require

A formal proof would need to characterize the loss
landscape of the transformer on loopy data and show
that the global minimum corresponds to loop-compensating
weights. This likely requires:

1. A precise characterization of the implicit factor
   graph G(W) as a function of the transformer weights W.

2. A characterization of the prediction error as a
   function of the KL divergence between the loopy BP
   fixed point and the true posterior.

3. A proof that the gradient of the prediction error
   with respect to W pushes W toward loop-compensating
   configurations.

Step 2 is the hardest. The relationship between
next-token prediction error and posterior KL divergence
is not simple — it involves the specific structure of
the output distribution and the nature of the task.

## Why This Matters

If the conjecture is true, it means trained transformers
are doing something more sophisticated than pure BP —
they are doing a form of variational inference that
accounts for loop correlations through weight adjustment.
This would explain why trained transformers perform well
on loopy real-world data despite the theoretical
inexactness of loopy BP.

It would also give a theoretical foundation for the
empirical observation that loopy BP works well in
practice on QBBN-structured graphs (see
EXPERIMENT_REPORT.md) — the weights are not pure BP
weights but loop-compensating weights that make the
Bethe approximation tighter.

## Status

Conjecture. Not proved. The bayes-learner result on
tree graphs is consistent with it. The loopy experiments
in this repo test convergence and correctness for fixed
weights but do not yet test whether backprop finds
loop-compensating weights. See experiments/plan.md
for proposed experiments that would test this directly.
